### Executive (turn 1)

I need a brief for a consultancy. Goal: reduce onboarding time. Make it professional.

### Assistant (turn 1)

Before I draft anything, I need real answers to five questions. We'll take them one at a time, starting with the most important.

"Reduce onboarding time" is a goal, not yet a brief. A consultancy handed that line will come back with a dozen questions, or worse, will fill the gaps with its own guesses. The goal is also still vague: "onboarding" could mean the first week, the first 90 days, or the time until a new hire works at full speed. The word "professional" does not fix that. What makes a brief professional is that nobody has to ask a follow-up.

**Question 1: Who is affected?** Which people or groups go through this onboarding, and how does the current process hurt them? Give me a role, a team or a headcount. For example: "about 40 new sales hires a year, who wait three weeks for system access."

### Executive (turn 2)

New technicians, mostly. It's too slow and everyone complains.

### Assistant (turn 2)

"New technicians" is a category, and "everyone complains" is a feeling. Neither tells a consultancy what to measure. I need a number or a name.

**Question 1, second try: Who is affected?** How many new technicians start in a year, and who has actually complained? A role is enough, for example "the field service managers who lose a technician's first six weeks" or "three of the last ten hires said they nearly quit". Give me the headcount and one specific example of the complaint.

### Executive (turn 3)

About 160 new technicians a year, out of a division of 1,200. The team leaders are the ones complaining that new people can't work alone for months. Georg's region is a disaster, honestly, he never signs anyone off.

### Assistant (turn 3)

Your answer on who is affected is good enough to move on. It gives a headcount (160 new technicians a year, about 13% of a division of 1,200) and a named group that complains (the team leaders). This works because a consultancy can size the problem from it.

"Can't work alone for months" is still a feeling until we have a number. I'll come back to it under "done means".

On Georg's region: one leader who "never signs anyone off" is a claim, not a finding. It might be the cause, or it might be one region with unusually strict standards, or a sign-off process that is unclear. I would change my mind if other regions show the same delay. I'll keep his name out of the brief and refer to "one region". A consultancy brief should say what we measured, not who we blame.

**Question 2: What happens today?** I need checked facts, not impressions. How long does it take today from a technician's first day to working alone, and how do you know? Tell me the number and where it comes from, such as an HR report, a sign-off date in a system, or an estimate from team leaders. If you don't have the number, say so, and we will write it into the brief as "unknown, to be measured by…".

### Executive (turn 4)

About 14 weeks on average before they work unsupervised. That number comes from the team leaders' sign-off dates in our system.

### Assistant (turn 4)

The 14 weeks is a strong answer. It has a number, a source and a system record behind it, and a consultancy can start from it.

There is one weakness. The number measures when team leaders signed people off, not when technicians could actually work alone. You told me earlier that one region has a leader who "never signs anyone off". If that is true, the 14-week average is partly a measure of sign-off habits, not of onboarding speed. If one region is far above the rest, the real problem may be the sign-off, not the onboarding. We can put this in the brief as a first task for the consultancy: "check whether sign-off dates reflect real readiness".

**Question 3: What should happen instead?** In the best realistic case, how many weeks should it take a new technician to work unsupervised, and what makes you think that target is possible? For example, "8 weeks, because our best region already does it in 9", or "10 weeks, because that is what competitors report". If you don't have a target, say so, and we will write "unknown, to be set after the first measurement".
