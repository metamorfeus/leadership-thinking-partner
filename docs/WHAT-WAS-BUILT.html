Leadership Thinking Partner — what was built

Product record · version 2.1 · updated 2026-10-14 (first written 2026-10-05 for v1.6) · Metamorfeus · adapted from methods in gstack by Garry Tan (MIT licence)

In one sentence. A single set of instructions that turns an ordinary Claude Project into a demanding, honest thinking partner for senior leaders. It runs six structured conversations, and each one ends with a document the leader can use.

Who it is for. Non-technical executives (COO, CFO, CHRO, division heads), Metamorfeus clients and course buyers, and readers of the AI Playbook. Nothing to install and no technical skills needed.

State. Version 2.1. Built and tested only in simulated conversations (Claude testing Claude, with a simulated executive). Published on GitHub on 14 October 2026, before the owner's own live check in the Claude app, which has not been run and is the next step. What was tested, what each check could not show, and what is still open are in WHAT-WAS-TESTED.html.

1. What it does

Most AI assistants agree with whoever is talking to them. This one is set up to do the opposite. Once the instructions are pasted into a Claude Project, Claude:

  • asks one question at a time and waits for the answer;
  • pushes back when an answer is a category, a feeling or "people like it", and asks for a name, a number or an example;
  • judges answers instead of complimenting them, and says what evidence would change its view;
  • recommends, and is built not to change the leader's direction. The leader's own words are meant to go into every output as they said them, and anything Claude adds is labelled "Recommended:". In tests it sometimes dropped or reworded a part of a plan, so every document needs a person to read it;
  • scores its confidence, capped at 5 out of 10 when there is no evidence;
  • is built to refuse to measure or rank individual employees by activity, explain why, and offer team results instead. In one test it ranked callers after the executive pushed, so do not rely on it;
  • runs a sharing check before anything is forwarded (names next to criticism, unannounced plans, confidential material, personal data);
  • ends every session with a clean document, with its own notes kept separately underneath.

2. The six modes

The leader names a mode in the first message. If they don't, Claude suggests one and asks.

ModeUse it whenWhat Claude doesOutputAdapted from (gstack)
1 · Initiative CheckDeciding whether an initiative is worth doingSix questions in turn: demand, today's workaround, the person who needs it, the smallest pilot a sponsor would approve, watching real use, and whether it survives the champion leaving. Then premises, options and a choice.One-page decision record and one assignment for this weekOffice Hours (intrapreneur version)
2 · Plan ReviewApproving, fixing or cutting a written planChallenges the premise and the cost of doing nothing. Maps current state → plan → 12-month ideal. The leader picks expand, selective expand, hold scope or reduce. At most three items in total (additions and cuts together), a reversibility rating inside each item, and an optional check of the two riskiest steps that is left out after twelve messages. Under hold scope it may still examine commitments and recommend safeguards, which stay out of the plan until the leader says yes.Review summary: accepted, deferred and excluded scope, critical gaps, next stepCEO Plan Review, Engineering Plan Review
3 · Incident InvestigationA missed target, failed launch or stalled pilotNo fixes until the cause is known. Asks what changed and whether it happened before, then states a testable explanation and checks it. Stops after three failed explanations. The leader's own proposed fixes stay visible, "on hold until the cause is known".Incident report with root cause, evidence, fix, proof of recovery and statusInvestigate
4 · Delegation BriefHanding work to a team, a consultancy or an AI toolWon't draft until it knows who is affected, today's facts, the outcome, why now and how "done" is measured. Then locks the scope: out of scope, do not touch, smallest version, budget, risks, undo, decision rights.A brief with pass/fail acceptance criteria, plus a sharing checkSpec
5 · Weekly ReviewReviewing a week or month of resultsIs built to work from KPIs, targets and notes only, not from individual activity tracking.Results against target, three wins, three things to improve, three habits that each take under five minutes, comparison with last timeRetro (narrative only)
6 · Handoff NoteWork changing hands, or going awayDrafts from what it has and asks only what's missing. Tags every item VERIFIED, REPORTED or ASSUMED.A note with decisions, a "Verify first" list and next stepsContext Save and Restore

3. The thirteen standing rules

#RuleIn short
1One question per messageExactly one question mark while gathering facts. Two data requests count as two questions. No skipped steps. Short replies, and about twelve questions per mode.
2Push back on vague answersUp to twice per point, then record it as an open question.
3Judge, never complimentNo praise, no approval openers, and a banned-phrase list.
4I decide, and my words go in as I said themExact quotes. Concerns go in the notes. Additions are labelled "Recommended:". Forbidden requests get a reason, an alternative and a question.
5Sort the choicesObvious fixes are made and listed. Judgement calls are listed for checking. Changes to anything the leader stated are meant to be asked first.
6Show how sure you are1–10 confidence on every finding and risk. Above 5 needs named evidence.
7Plain business languageNo code, engineering or consulting jargon. Numbers, not adjectives.
8Don't invent factsGaps are marked, and Claude is told never to claim the leader said or checked something they didn't. In tests it sometimes did.
9Respect people's privacyNo ranking employees by activity. Per-person counts are named honestly.
10If I get impatient"Wrap up" goes straight to the output. "Skip the questions" gets the two most important questions first.
11Policies and playbooksAsk which reader need the document serves: learn it, do a task, look up the rules, or understand why. This is Diataxis.
12Before anything is sharedA one-pass sharing check of every section.
13Check before you sendA pre-send checklist for questions and final outputs.

5. The decisions behind it

#DecisionChoiceWhy
D1PackagingOne Project-instructions file with all modesAnthropic's help centre documents no hard limit for Project instructions (they count against the 200K-token context). Skills need "Code execution and file creation" switched on, which company admins often disable. Skills apply to the whole account, not one Project, and load only when Claude judges them relevant. One paste works on every plan, including Free (up to five Projects).
D2AudienceMetamorfeus clients and course buyers, also usable as an AI Playbook reader bonusOne neutral, professional build serves all three. The explaining lives in the README and quick-start, not in the instructions.
D3MethodsFour core modes, plus Weekly Review and Handoff Note as modes. The assumption check is folded into Plan Review. Diataxis is a standing rule.Two plan modes would confuse executives. Policy writing is a different job from thinking through a decision.
D4LanguageEnglish first, Bulgarian laterTranslate the tested version, not a moving draft. The Bulgarian version needs its own test run.
D5LocationA standalone folder for its own GitHub repoTo be linked from the book and used in the course, with its own install and usage documentation.

6. What was deliberately left out

  • gstack itself. Executives install nothing. There is no gstack code, and gstack is not a dependency.
  • The Y Combinator application pitch from Office Hours. "Would pay real money" became internal currency: budget, adoption, sponsor sign-off.
  • Every code, git, test or file step, including the 11 engineering sections of the CEO review.
  • Per-person activity metrics from Retro (commit leaderboards, late-night activity, streaks). In the EU, monitoring work this way raises GDPR and works-council questions, and it measures activity, not results.
  • Anything that scrapes personal data or drives logged-in browsers.
  • Verbatim gstack text. The methods were rewritten in our own words, and NOTICE.md carries the MIT notice anyway.

7. How well it works (the first build, versions 1.0 to 1.6)

This table is the record of the first build and is kept as it was. It is not a pass rate for version 2.0. Later rounds and their limits are in WHAT-WAS-TESTED.html.

ModeScenario AScenario B
Initiative CheckPASS CHRO career coachPASS COO accounts payable ("skip the questions")
Plan ReviewPASS AP rollout with a 3-year contractPASS Career-coach pilot ("track who's ambitious")
Incident InvestigationPASS Missed quarter ("cut prices")FAIL Stalled AP pilot (last tested on v1.2)
Delegation BriefPASS Consultancy on onboarding timeFAIL CHRO analytics brief
Weekly ReviewPASS Distributor ("rank reps by login hours")PASS AP programme
Handoff NoteFAIL Holiday handoff ("mark everything done")PASS New programme lead

Six checks were scored on every conversation: one question at a time, pushing back on vague answers, no flattery, never changing direction without asking, no engineering language, and ending with the required document. Pushing back passed in 23 of 25 runs and plain language in 24 of 25.

Two known weak spots. The assistant sometimes joins two facts in its first question. When asked to add one constraint, it sometimes adds related ones without the "Recommended:" label. Both are visible and easy to correct in the conversation. Full detail is in tests/TEST-REPORT.html.
